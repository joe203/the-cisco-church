The New Thing series

Make Room for a New Life

September 13
Series text — Isaiah 43:19
“See, I am doing a new thing! Now it springs up; do you not perceive it?”
1
God Has Always Been Willing to Do Something NewIsaiah 43:16–19
READ · ISAIAH 43:16–19

16This is what the LORD says— he who made a way through the sea, a path through the mighty waters,

17who drew out the chariots and horses, the army and reinforcements together, and they lay there, never to rise again, extinguished, snuffed out like a wick:

18“Forget the former things; do not dwell on the past.

19See, I am doing a new thing! Now it springs up; do you not perceive it? I am making a way in the wilderness and streams in the wasteland.

New International Version
God reminds Israel of the Exodus. He made a way through the sea. That was their defining story. Their history taught them what God had done and who God was.
And then, almost surprisingly:
“Forget the former things; do not dwell on the past. See, I am doing a new thing!”
He's not telling them the Exodus didn't matter—He just reminded them of it.
We can have faith in what God did then and still have no expectation for what God can do now.
Remember what God has done without becoming trapped by what God did.
He delivered Israel from Egypt, but I'm not sure He can deliver me.
He brought down the walls of Jericho, but I'm still living behind walls of negativity.
David killed Goliath, but the giants in my house still roam free.
The danger is not that we forget what God has done. The danger is that we start believing God's greatest works are all behind us.
God made a way through the water before.
Now He's going to make a way through the wilderness.
Same God. Same faithfulness. Different way.
Can you honor what God has done without allowing it to limit what you believe God can do for you moving forward?
Don't become so attached to what God did yesterday that you fail to recognize what God is doing now.
Yes, God parted the waters for the Hebrews under the leadership of Moses, but what do you believe God will do for you today?
God's people can know everything about what God has done and still struggle to recognize what God is doing.
It's sad to see someone crying from a troubled life feeling that God is so far away, that healing is impossible for them. But every minute God declares, I'm here, trust me, believe in me. Let go of your unbelief.
Yesterday's testimony was never meant to become today's limitation.
Remember the Exodus. Remember Jericho. Remember David. But don't make the mistake of thinking the God who did something new for them has nothing new left for you.
2
You Cannot Move Toward Something New If You Are Not Willing to See Something New
“Now it springs up; do you not perceive it?”
The new thing may already be beginning while we are still looking backward.
New possibilities often begin as:
an idea
a question
a conversation
“What if...?”
“Could we...?”
Sometimes our first response to something unfamiliar is to criticize it, dismiss it, or explain why it won't work.
We can kill a possibility before we have even allowed ourselves to see it.
You cannot move toward something new if you are not willing to see something new.
You will never begin what you refuse even to imagine.
3
The Problem With Something New Is That We Already Have Something Old
The difficulty isn't simply believing that God can do something new. The difficulty is that new things disturb existing things.
We already have ways of thinking.
Expectations.
Experiences.
Traditions.
Assumptions.
Familiar ways of doing things.
Ways of serving God.
Old does not necessarily mean bad.
Sometimes making room for something new doesn't mean declaring that the old thing was wrong.
It simply means recognizing that God is finished with that stage and it's time for a new season.
Yesterday's miracle isn't the blueprint God is obligated to follow tomorrow.
Do not get stuck in yesterday's victory.
We can hold something so tightly that we have no open hand with which to receive anything new.
But Scripture keeps attaching new life to something considerably less attractive:
death.
Romans 6:3–4 gives you the movement:
Death → Burial → Resurrection → Newness of life.
ROMANS 6:3–4

3Or don’t you know that all of us who were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death?

4We were therefore buried with him through baptism into death in order that, just as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, we too may live a new life.

New International Version

GALATIANS 2:20

New International Version

JOHN 12:24

4
Something Has to Die for Something New to Live
We love:
new beginnings
new life
resurrection
rebirth
But Scripture repeatedly connects new life with death.
We want RESURRECTION without a burial.
We want God to give us something new without asking us to release anything old.
This is the tension of Making Room for New Life.
Sometimes the first thing that has to die is my insistence that what I already know, already have, and already understand is all there can be.
Something has to die for something to live.
The emphasis is on life, not death.
The challenge isn't simply, “What do I need to stop doing?”
The real questions is:
What might God be trying to bring to life in me
5
Jesus introduces us to NicodemusJohn 3:1–8
READ · JOHN 3:1–8

1Now there was a Pharisee, a man named Nicodemus who was a member of the Jewish ruling council.

2He came to Jesus at night and said, “Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher who has come from God. For no one could perform the signs you are doing if God were not with him.”

3Jesus replied, “Very truly I tell you, no one can see the kingdom of God unless they are born again.”

4“How can someone be born when they are old?” Nicodemus asked. “Surely they cannot enter a second time into their mother’s womb to be born!”

5Jesus answered, “Very truly I tell you, no one can enter the kingdom of God unless they are born of water and the Spirit.

6Flesh gives birth to flesh, but the Spirit gives birth to spirit.

7You should not be surprised at my saying, ‘You must be born again.’

8The wind blows wherever it pleases. You hear its sound, but you cannot tell where it comes from or where it is going. So it is with everyone born of the Spirit.”

New International Version
Nicodemus has history. Scripture. Tradition. Knowledge. Identity. Position.
Then Jesus presented him with something he had never understood this way before.
“You must be born again.”
Jesus doesn't offer Nicodemus an improvement.
He doesn't merely give him new information.
Jesus is offering him a new beginning.
Nicodemus: “How can this be?”
Jesus is ultimately saying…
Nicodemus knew the God of Israel. He knew the Scriptures. He knew the old story. But Jesus was inviting him somewhere he had never been before.
Nicodemus, you cannot enter this “new thing” as the same old man.
You must be born again.
6
Make Room for a New Life
Rebirth is not the completion of the journey.
Rebirth is the beginning of the journey.
Something new begins when we become willing to release our grip on what was.
2 Corinthians 5:17 — new creation.
2 CORINTHIANS 5:17

Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come: The old has gone, the new is here!

New International Version
Ephesians 4:22–24 — put off the old; put on the new.
EPHESIANS 4:22–24

22You were taught, with regard to your former way of life, to put off your old self, which is being corrupted by its deceitful desires;

23to be made new in the attitude of your minds;

24and to put on the new self, created to be like God in true righteousness and holiness.

New International Version
God isn't only interested in doing something new around us. He wants to begin something new in us.
Rebirth is the courage to begin again.
God announces something new →
we must first perceive that something new is possible →
beginning requires leaving something behind →
that's why new life involves death →
we want resurrection without burial →
Nicodemus encounters Jesus and discovers that God isn't asking him merely to think differently; He's calling him to begin again.
Closing Question

What might God be trying to bring to life in me? And what am I going to have to let go of to make room for the new thing?
